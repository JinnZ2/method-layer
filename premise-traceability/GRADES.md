# EPISTEMIC GRADE LEGEND

CC0. Extracted from chain-audit-b5.md so the grades are reusable across
domains and citable by other instruments.

Grades describe the ORIGIN of a claim, not confidence in it. A [FP] can
be excellent. An [INST] can be narrow.

  [FP]    FIRST PRINCIPLE
          Asserted as a starting point. Not derived, not measured.
          Held because the structure requires a starting point.

  [INST]  INSTRUMENTED
          Directly measured, with a stated instrument and a stated
          resolution.

  [INF-I] INFORMED BY INSTRUMENT
          A measurement exists, but the claim is broader than what the
          instrument covers. THE GAP BETWEEN MEASURED RANGE AND CLAIMED
          RANGE IS THE EXPOSURE. Record both ranges where known; where
          the tested range is unknown, that is NOT MEASURED, not zero.

  [INF]   INFERRED
          Derived from other links. Carries every dependency above it.
          No independent support.

  [CONV]  CONVENTION
          Adopted because it made the mathematics work. No truth claim
          was ever attached, and one accreted anyway.

## USE

  1. Grade every link in a chain, not just the disputed ones. The
     finding is usually in the ungraded majority.
  2. Grades DO NOT TRAVEL. A premise crossing a field boundary arrives
     ungraded and is typically received at a higher grade than it left.
     That upgrade is the PROMOTION ERROR. Watch for it at branch forks
     and at field boundaries.
  3. A chain with no [INST] link after its first is a deduction
     structure, not an empirical one. Say so explicitly rather than
     letting the reader assume measurement somewhere in the middle.
  4. When grading someone else's chain, mark that the grades are yours
     and not the field's. A reader should re-derive them rather than
     cite them.
